Rule / OM Reference: Rule 8 and Rule 10, CCS (LTC) Rules, 1988; DoPT O.M. No. 31011/12/2007-Estt.(A) dated November 29, 2007.
  1. The concession to visit "Any Place in India" (All India LTC / AILTC) is admissible once in a four-year calendar block (e.g., 2022–2025, 2026–2029).
  2. The four-year block is divided into two 2-year sub-blocks (e.g., Sub-block 1: 2022–2023; Sub-block 2: 2024–2025). In each sub-block, a Government Servant (GS) is entitled to Home Town LTC.
  3. Under Rule 8(2), AILTC can be availed against (and in lieu of) one of the two Home Town concessions in the 4-year block.
  4. The Grace Year (Rule 10): A concession not availed in a given 2-year or 4-year block may be carried forward and availed in the first year of the succeeding block (the "Grace Year").
    • Example: If Home Town LTC for sub-block 2022–2023 is unutilized, it can be availed up to December 31, 2024.
    • Example: If AILTC for the 2022–2025 block is unutilized, it can be carried over and availed up to December 31, 2026.
  5. Outward Journey Rule: To legally establish carry-over utilization, the outward journey must commence on or before December 31 of the grace year. The return journey may legally fall in the subsequent calendar year without forfeiting the concession.
Rule / OM Reference: Rule 10, CCS (LTC) Rules, 1988; DoPT O.M. No. 31011/7/2013-Estt.(A-IV) dated September 26, 2014.
  1. An employee carrying over an unutilized LTC from a previous block into a grace year may avail both the carried-forward concession and the concession due for the current block during the same calendar year, provided they are not undertaken on the identical continuous spell of leave.
  2. Order of Depletion: The carried-over LTC must be exhausted first. If an employee performs an LTC journey in the grace year without explicitly declaring it against the carried-forward block, administrative processing by default debits the older, lapsing concession first to prevent financial detriment.
  3. Lapse Principle: If the outward journey of the carried-over concession does not commence by 23:59 hours on December 31 of the grace year, the concession lapses permanently. An administrative authority possesses no statutory power to relax this rule without express concurrence from the DoPT and Department of Expenditure.
Rule / OM Reference: Rule 8, Note 2 of CCS (LTC) Rules, 1988, inserted vide DoPT Notification No. 31011/7/2013-Estt.(A-IV) dated September 26, 2014.

Fresh recruits entering Central Government service are entitled to LTC on a yearly basis for the first 8 continuous years of service:

Year 1: Home Town
Year 2: Home Town
Year 3: Home Town
Year 4: All India (AILTC)
Year 5: Home Town
Year 6: Home Town
Year 7: Home Town
Year 8: All India (AILTC)
  • Grace Year Inapplicability: The carry-over grace year facility under Rule 10 is not applicable during the 8-year fresh recruit cycle. Concessions must be availed within the specific 12-month anniversary/calendar window or they lapse.
  • Transition Mechanics: After completing 8 full years, the employee merges directly into the prevailing national 4-year block. If the 8th year ends mid-way through a regular nationwide 4-year block, the employee is granted entitlement for the remaining sub-blocks in accordance with DoPT date-of-joining matrices.
Rule / OM Reference: Rule 5, CCS (LTC) Rules, 1988; Ministry of Home Affairs O.M. No. 43/15/57-Ests.(A) dated June 24, 1958.
  1. The Home Town declared by a Government servant on initial appointment is verified and recorded in the Service Book based on specific criteria: permanent residence of parents, ownership of immovable property, agricultural ties, or ancestral origin.
  2. One-Time Alteration: Under Rule 5(2), the declaration of Home Town once made is normally treated as final. However, the Head of Department (HoD) may allow an alteration only once in the entire service career, provided the employee establishes that circumstances have materially changed (e.g., permanent acquisition of residential property, total severance of connections from ancestral village, relocation of living parents).
  3. An employee cannot switch Home Town back and forth to exploit travel distance reimbursements under Home Town LTC.
Rule / OM Reference: Rule 4(d), CCS (LTC) Rules, 1988; DoPT O.M. No. 31011/4/2008-Estt.(A) dated September 23, 2008; clarified vide O.M. No. 11012/1/2016-Estt.A-IV dated June 2, 2016.
  1. A member of the family is deemed "wholly dependent" on the Government servant if their total monthly income from all sources (including pension, stipend, commercial property rentals, and agricultural returns) does not exceed the minimum family pension admissible under the Central Government, plus the Dearness Relief (DR) sanctioned thereon.
  2. Following the 7th CPC, the minimum family pension is fixed at ₹9,000 per month.
  3. Operational Income Formula:
    $$\text{Income Ceiling} = ₹9,000 + \left(₹9,000 \times \frac{\text{Current Rate of DR}}{100}\right)$$
  4. If an employee's parent receives a basic pension of ₹9,000 and DR at the current rate, their total income equals the threshold. However, if their basic pension or outside income is even ₹9,001 (excluding DR), or if total income exceeds the ceiling calculated on the date the journey commences, dependency is legally severed, and the LTC claim for that dependent must be disallowed.
Rule / OM Reference: Rule 4(d), CCS (LTC) Rules, 1988; DoPT O.M. No. 31011/4/2008-Estt.(A) dated September 23, 2008.
  • Unmarried Son: Admissible irrespective of age, provided he is wholly dependent on the GS, resides with them, and his monthly income does not exceed the ₹9,000 + DR threshold.
  • Daughter: Unmarried daughters of any age are covered, provided they are dependent.
  • Divorced / Widowed / Deserted Daughters: Fully eligible to be treated as members of the family for LTC, irrespective of age, subject to two binding conditions:
    1. They must reside with the Government servant.
    2. Their aggregate monthly income (including maintenance, family pension from late husband, or child support) does not exceed the dependency income ceiling ($₹9,000 + \text{DR}$).
  • Children of Widowed/Divorced Daughters: Dependent minor children of a widowed or divorced daughter residing with the GS are also covered under the definition of family.
Rule / OM Reference: Rule 4(d), Explanation 2, CCS (LTC) Rules, 1988; DoPT O.M. No. 31011/3/2014-Estt.(A-IV) dated May 14, 2014.
  1. The concession is restricted to two surviving unmarried children or stepchildren.
  2. Statutory Exceptions:
    • The restriction does not apply in respect of children born prior to October 20, 1997.
    • Where the first childbirth results in a single child, and the subsequent second delivery results in multiple births (twins or triplets), all children born out of the subsequent delivery are eligible for LTC (total 3 or 4 children allowed).
  3. The Inverse Disqualification Scenario: If an employee has twins in the first delivery (resulting in 2 surviving children), and subsequently has a third child from a second pregnancy, the third child is strictly ineligible for LTC.
Rule / OM Reference: Rule 8(3), CCS (LTC) Rules, 1988; DoPT O.M. No. 31011/8/89-Estt.(A) dated May 8, 1990.
  1. Joint Declaration: The husband and wife must declare to their respective Controlling Officers who will claim LTC for the family unit, or whether they will claim separately as independent Government servants.
  2. Option A (Treated as Independent Employees): Each spouse may claim for themselves and their own dependent parents/siblings. Spouses cannot claim for each other simultaneously. Children can be claimed in the bill of either spouse, but never both for the same sub-block/block.
  3. Option B (Treated as One Family Unit): One spouse claims LTC as the primary Government servant; the other spouse travels as a dependent family member. The non-claiming spouse cannot claim an advance, leave encashment, or independent travel allowance from their own department for that block.
  4. Home Town Disparity: If the spouses have different declared Home Towns, either spouse can declare the other's Home Town as their own for the purpose of Home Town LTC, or they may visit their respective Home Towns independently under their own entitlement.
Rule / OM Reference: Ministry of Finance, DoE O.M. No. 19030/1/2017-E.IV dated July 13, 2017; DoPT O.M. No. 31011/8/2017-Estt.A-IV dated September 19, 2017.

Rule / OM Reference: DoPT O.M. No. 31011/3/2016-Estt.(A-IV) dated September 19, 2017; Ministry of Railways Commercial Circulars.
  1. Dynamic/Flexi-fares: Reimbursable on LTC for entitled classes in trains where flexi-fare systems are active (Rajdhani, Shatabdi, Duronto), provided the ticket is booked in the employee's entitled travel class.
  2. Vande Bharat & Tejas Express:
    • Pay Levels 6 to 11 are entitled to AC Chair Car (CC).
    • Pay Levels 12 and above are entitled to Executive Chair Car (EC).
  3. If an employee eligible only for AC 3-Tier travels in Vande Bharat (which has only CC and EC), they are permitted travel in AC Chair Car, and the fare is fully reimbursable.
  4. Catering Charges: Mandatory catering charges billed alongside the railway ticket in Rajdhani, Shatabdi, Vande Bharat, and Duronto trains form part of the basic transportation cost and are reimbursable.
Rule / OM Reference: Ministry of Finance, DoE O.M. No. 19024/03/2021-E.IV dated June 16, 2022; DoPT O.M. No. 31011/12/2022-Estt.A-IV dated August 29, 2022; DoPT O.M. No. 31011/11/2023-Pers.Policy A-IV dated October 20, 2023.
  1. The Three Authorized Travel Agents (ATAs): Air tickets on LTC must be purchased exclusively from:
    • M/s Balmer Lawrie & Company Limited (BLCL)
    • M/s Ashok Travels & Tours (ATT)
    • Indian Railway Catering and Tourism Corporation Ltd. (IRCTC)
  2. Booking Direct via Airline Portals or Private Portals: Strictly prohibited. Any booking made directly from airline websites or third-party aggregators (MakeMyTrip, EaseMyTrip, Yatra, Cleartrip) results in immediate rejection of the entire airfare claim unless explicitly relaxed by DoPT under special verification procedures.
  3. Cheapest Fare in the 3-Hour Slot: The employee must search for flights in a 3-hour time slot on the intended date of departure (e.g., 06:00–09:00, 09:00–12:00). The flight offering the Best Available Fare (cheapest) within that slot must be selected, with an allowable optimization margin within a 10% price band for non-stop options. The employee MUST print/save the flight search results page showing fare comparisons and submit it with the LTC bill.
  4. The 21-Day Advance Booking Provision: Employees are advised to book tickets at least 21 days before departure. Bookings made within less than 72 hours of departure require a self-declared written justification countersigned by the Competent Authority.
Rule / OM Reference: DoPT O.M. No. 31011/15/2022-Estt.A-IV dated September 9, 2026 (extending the scheme for two years through September 25, 2028).
  1. Conversion Provision: All eligible Central Government employees may convert one Home Town LTC in a 4-year block to visit North Eastern Region (NER), UT of Jammu & Kashmir, UT of Ladakh, or Andaman & Nicobar Islands (A&N).
  2. Air Travel Relaxation for Non-Entitled Employees (Level 5 and below): Permitted to fly Economy Class only on the designated hub-to-destination sector:
    • To NER: Between Kolkata/Guwahati and any place in NER.
    • To J&K / Ladakh: Between Delhi/Amritsar and any place in J&K/Ladakh.
    • To A&N: Between Kolkata/Chennai/Visakhapatnam and Port Blair.
    The journey from employee Headquarters to the designated hub (e.g., HQ to Delhi or Kolkata) must be performed by their entitled train class.
  3. Fresh Recruits Bonus: Fresh recruits are allowed an additional conversion of one of their three Home Town LTCs in a block to visit UT of J&K or UT of Ladakh.
  4. Home Town Restriction: An employee whose declared Home Town is situated in one of these four regions cannot avail conversion to visit the region where their own Home Town is located.
Rule / OM Reference: Rule 14 and Rule 15, CCS (LTC) Rules, 1988.
  1. Amount of Advance: Limited to 90% of the estimated total outward and return fare for both the Government servant and entitled family members.
  2. Drawal Timelines: Advance can be drawn up to 125 days (or prevailing Railway ARP window) before the proposed date of journey by rail, and 30 days before departure for journeys by air/road/sea.
  3. The 10-Day Ticket Verification Deadline (Rule 14(4)): Once the advance is credited, the employee must purchase tickets and produce railway PNRs or air tickets to the Controlling Officer within 10 days of drawing the advance. Failure to produce proof within 10 days triggers immediate lump-sum recovery of the entire advance.
Rule / OM Reference: Rule 14, CCS (LTC) Rules, 1988; Rule 258 of General Financial Rules (GFR), 2017; DoPT O.M. No. 31011/11/2023-Pers.Policy A-IV.
  1. The Penal Rate: Charged at 2% over the prevailing General Provident Fund (GPF) interest rate applicable during the period of default:
    $$\text{Penal Interest Rate} = \text{Prevailing GPF Rate (\%)} + 2.0\%$$
  2. Charge Period: Calculated from the exact date of disbursement of the advance from the Government treasury to the date of actual recovery/refund into the Government account.
  3. Triggers for Penal Interest: Unutilized advance retained beyond 10 days without buying tickets, failure to deposit ticket cancellation proceeds immediately, or failure to submit the adjustment bill within 1 month of completing the return journey.
Rule / OM Reference: Rule 38-A of CCS (Leave) Rules, 1972; DoPT O.M. No. 14028/4/2009-Estt.(L) dated June 3, 2009.
  1. Encashment Quantum: Up to 10 days of Earned Leave (EL) at the time of availing LTC (either Home Town or All India), without any linkage to the actual number of days or nature of leave availed for the journey.
  2. The 60-Day Lifetime Ceiling: Total EL encashed during the entire service career under LTC cannot exceed 60 days (which is independent of the 300-day superannuation EL encashment ceiling).
  3. The 30-Day Mandatory Residue Rule: After deducting the 10 days of encashment and the actual leave days sanctioned for the journey, a minimum balance of at least 30 days of Earned Leave must remain in the employee's leave ledger.
  4. Encashment Formula:
    $$\text{Encashment Amount} = \frac{\text{Basic Pay} + \text{Dearness Allowance (DA)}}{30} \times 10$$
    (HRA and Transport Allowance are strictly excluded).
Rule / OM Reference: Rule 14 and Rule 15, CCS (LTC) Rules, 1988; DoPT O.M. No. 31011/3/2015-Estt.(A-IV) dated May 13, 2016.
Case A: Advance WAS Drawn

Submit claim within 1 MONTH of return journey completion. Failure leads to immediate lump-sum recovery of advance + Penal Interest (GPF + 2%).

Case B: Advance NOT Drawn

Submit claim within 3 MONTHS of return journey completion. Failure results in automatic forfeiture unless condoned by HoD/Ministry.

Condonation Powers: With the concurrence of the Financial Adviser (FA), the Head of Department/Ministry can relax the limit up to 3 months (where advance was drawn) and 6 months (where no advance was drawn) if the employee faced circumstances beyond their control.

Rule / OM Reference: DoPT O.M. No. 31011/3/2018-Estt.(A-IV) dated June 20, 2018; O.M. dated September 27, 2022.
  1. Submission of original physical or digital boarding passes along with the LTC adjustment bill is mandatory to verify performance of air travel.
  2. Procedure upon Loss: The employee must obtain a formal Boarding Certificate / Passenger Manifest Verification Certificate issued by the concerned airline confirming that the passenger actually boarded and flew the specified flight on that date, accompanied by a self-declaration affidavit.
  3. Administrative Condonation: The Financial Adviser / Head of Department (HoD) is empowered to accept the alternative airline certificate in lieu of the missing boarding pass without referring the case to DoPT, provided tickets were booked through BLCL/ATT/IRCTC within the rules.
Rule / OM Reference: Rule 7, CCS (LTC) Rules, 1988; DoPT Compilation of Administrative Orders.
  • Under Suspension: A suspended employee cannot avail LTC for themselves as they cannot be granted leave. However, their entitled family members are permitted to avail LTC independently during the suspension period.
  • Study Leave: Fully admissible for both self and family. Reimbursement is restricted to the fare between the place of study and destination, or Headquarters and destination, whichever is less.
  • Child Care Leave (CCL) & Maternity Leave: Fully admissible. LTC can be availed during any nature of sanctioned leave, including Casual Leave (CL), Special CL, Maternity Leave, Paternity Leave, and CCL.
  • Extra Ordinary Leave (EOL): Admissible during sanctioned EOL without medical certificate, provided the continuous absence does not breach maximum leave limits.
Rule / OM Reference: Rule 16, CCS (LTC) Rules, 1988; CCS (CCA) Rules, 1965.
  1. Suspension of Future Concessions (Rule 16(1)): If a Disciplinary Authority has reason to believe an employee has preferred a fraudulent claim, the employee shall not be allowed to avail the next two sets of LTC (one to Home Town and one to Any Place in India) pending completion of inquiry.
  2. Mandatory Debarment upon Conviction in Inquiry (Rule 16(2)): If found guilty of misuse or fraud in departmental proceedings, the employee shall be permanently debarred from availing those two sets of LTC, in addition to any major or minor penalty imposed under the CCS (CCA) Rules, 1965 (such as reduction in pay stage, withholding of increments, or dismissal).
  3. Complete Financial Recovery: Disallowance of the entire tainted bill and immediate recovery of any drawn advance or leave encashment with penal interest ($GPF + 2\%$).
Rule / OM Reference: Rule 12 and Rule 13, CCS (LTC) Rules, 1988; DoPT O.M. No. 31011/3/2009-Estt.(A) dated October 28, 2009; Clarified vide DoPT O.M. No. 31011/06/2023-Estt.A-IV dated March 29, 2023.
  1. General Prohibition on Private Vehicles/Taxis: Travel by private car, chartered vehicle, or hired taxi between places connected by a direct rail link is strictly non-reimbursable for the travel fare component.
  2. Places Not Connected by Rail: If the destination or part of the journey is not connected by rail, travel must be undertaken by public transport operated by the Central/State Government, local bodies, or a State Roadways Transport Corporation (SRTC). If performed by taxi/auto-rickshaw where public buses operate, reimbursement is restricted strictly to the entitled public bus fare or actuals, whichever is less.
  3. Disability Exemption: Use of an owned car or hired taxi for the entire LTC journey is permissible on grounds of severe disability of the Government servant or an eligible dependent family member, provided it is supported by a valid medical certificate and prior sanction is obtained from the Head of Department (HoD). Reimbursement is restricted to the entitled train fare via the shortest direct route.
  4. The "Nil Claim" & Leave Encashment Workaround (March 2023 OM): If an employee travels entirely by private car/taxi (where fare reimbursement is impermissible), they can submit a "Nil Fare Claim" accompanied by proof of visit (e.g., hotel bills, toll receipts, self-declaration). This formally debits the LTC block and validates the 10-day Earned Leave encashment without violating audit standards.
Rule / OM Reference: Rule 13, CCS (LTC) Rules, 1988; Supplementary Rule (SR) 30; DoPT O.M. No. 31011/18/2023-Estt.A-IV dated February 4, 2021.
  1. Point-to-Point Direct Mandate: Reimbursement is allowed only for a journey performed on a point-to-point through-ticket over the shortest direct route between Headquarters and the declared destination.
  2. Circuitous / Split Journeys: An employee is permitted to take a circuitous route or make stopovers (e.g., $\text{Delhi} \rightarrow \text{Jaipur} \rightarrow \text{Goa}$ instead of $\text{Delhi} \rightarrow \text{Goa}$ direct), but financial reimbursement is capped at the entitled class fare of the shortest direct route between HQ and the final declared destination.
  3. Multi-Leg Tickets: If tickets are bought in pieces (broken journeys), the aggregate reimbursed amount cannot exceed the through-fare of the direct line. Any extra distance, layover expenses, or local conveyance incurred during intermediate halts is borne entirely by the employee.
Rule / OM Reference: Rule 8(1) and Rule 9, CCS (LTC) Rules, 1988; DoPT O.M. No. 31011/14/86-Estt.(A) dated May 8, 1987.
  1. Independent Movement: Family members need not travel together with the Government servant. They may travel in different groups at different times during the same 2-year or 4-year block, and may visit completely different destinations during an All India LTC block.
  2. Family Traveling from Origin other than HQ: If family members reside away from the employee's Headquarters (e.g., dependent parents living in the ancestral home), they can travel from their place of residence to the declared destination and back. However, the reimbursement is strictly restricted to the fare from Headquarters to the destination via the shortest route, or the actual fare paid, whichever is lower.
  3. Separate Claims & Advances: Separate advances and adjustment claims can be processed for each distinct travel group, provided the respective deadlines (1 month with advance, 3 months without advance) are maintained from the date of completion of that specific group's return journey.
Rule / OM Reference: Ministry of Home Affairs / DoPT O.M. No. 31011/8/89-Estt.(A) dated May 8, 1990; Supplementary Rules.
  1. LTC in Combination with Official Tour: If an employee proceeds on an official tour and avails sanctioned leave immediately following the assignment to undertake LTC, travel from Headquarters to the tour station is regulated as Travelling Allowance (TA) on tour. Travel from the tour station to the LTC destination and back to Headquarters is regulated under LTC Rules, restricted to the fare from the LTC destination directly to HQ.
  2. LTC in Combination with Transfer: If an employee is transferred and avails LTC while in transit, the employee's family can perform the LTC journey from the old HQ to the new HQ via the declared LTC destination:
    $$\text{Reimbursement} = \min\Big(\text{Actual Travel Cost},\; (\text{Transfer TA Entitlement} + \text{LTC Entitlement from Old HQ to LTC Destination and back})\Big)$$
Rule / OM Reference: Rule 7, CCS (LTC) Rules, 1988; MHA O.M. No. 31011/1/77-Ests.(A) dated October 1, 1977.
  1. Leave Preparatory to Retirement (LPR): An employee on LPR can avail both Home Town and All India LTC, provided the entire journey (including the return journey) is completed before the date of superannuation / expiry of LPR. No reimbursement is admissible for any travel leg performed after midnight of the retirement date.
  2. Resignation: If an employee avails LTC while on leave and tenders their resignation without returning to active duty, the entire concession is invalidated. The full advance or reimbursed amount must be refunded immediately.
Rule / OM Reference: Rule 12(1) and Rule 13, Note 2, CCS (LTC) Rules, 1988; DoPT O.M. No. 31011/8/1998-Estt.(A) dated March 31, 1999.
  1. Traveling in a Higher Class: If an employee travels in a higher class than entitled (e.g., a Level 5 employee traveling by AC 2-Tier or Air), reimbursement is strictly restricted to the fare of their entitled class (e.g., Sleeper Class train fare) by the shortest direct route, provided the ticket was booked via an authorized channel/ATA.
  2. Traveling in a Lower Class: If an employee travels in a lower class than entitled (e.g., a Level 11 employee traveling in AC 3-Tier instead of AC 2-Tier), reimbursement is restricted to the actual fare paid for the lower class. The employee cannot claim the difference as a savings bonus.
